Where Homeowner Responsibility Ends & County Service Begins

In Collier County and City of Naples utilities, property owners own and maintain the private sewer lateral from the interior plumbing stack all the way to the municipal sewer cleanout or street main connection.

If sewage is backing up into your home, the County will not dispatch service crews unless you have verified with a licensed plumber that the blockage is situated inside the public main. Our high-definition cameras capture timestamped, calibrated distance footage that serves as definitive proof if the blockage sits on the county-maintained side of the property line.

Private Sewer Lateral (Homeowner):

All indoor branch lines, under-slab building drains, and the exterior yard line up to the property easement boundary.

Public Sewer Collection Main (County):

The central municipal sewer main located in the street asphalt or public utility easement corridor.

Regional Plumbing Science

Environmental & Geological Factors Impacting Naples Plumbing

Drain cleaning in Southwest Florida requires understanding our unique subtropical environment, including tidal backpressure, fine carbonate shell sands, and root hydrology.

1. High Coastal Groundwater Tables

During summer rainy months (June through September), Collier County's groundwater table rises to within inches of the ground surface. Saturated sub-base soils exert immense hydrostatic pressure on underground pipe joints. Any minor unsealed seam becomes an entry path for surrounding groundwater and fine sandy silt to infiltrate your lateral.

2. Aggressive Subtropical Tree Hydrology

Landscaping throughout Old Naples, Pelican Bay, and Port Royal features dense hedges of Ficus benjamina and Ficus microcarpa, alongside towering royal and coconut palms. During dry winter months, these trees seek out moisture vapor escaping from sewer pipes, invading joints with dense root webs that cause chronic toilet and mainline backups.

3. Seasonal Snowbird Plumbing Dormancy

With over 40% of Naples residences vacant during the summer, drainage systems sit dormant for months. Grease and soap scum harden like cement in dry horizontal pipes, and P-traps evaporate completely, allowing sewer gas to infiltrate homes. Our seasonal arrival flushing re-establishes healthy flow before normal daily water use resumes.
