While You Wait

What to Do Before Your Technician Arrives

1. Stop Running Water

Do not run dishwashers, washing machines, or flush toilets. If the main sewer line is restricted, adding more water forces sewage into low-lying shower and tub drains.

2. Avoid Liquid Chemical Drain Openers

Do not pour store-bought acids or lye into the drain. They will not clear a main backup, can damage pipes, and create a toxic splashing hazard for technicians.

3. Clear Cleanout Access

If you know where your exterior cleanout is located in your yard or flowerbed, ensure the area is accessible and pets are safely secured indoors.

1. Immediate Preparation Steps While You Wait

If you are currently experiencing an active plumbing backup, taking these immediate precautions will protect your home from unnecessary water damage:

  • Cease Water Consumption: Do not run water in sinks, operate the dishwasher, or flush toilets. In multi-story residences, water drained upstairs will gravity-feed directly into ground-floor bathtubs and floor drains.
  • Do Not Use Chemical Cleaners: Avoid pouring chemical liquid cleaners into standing water. Caustic acids generate high heat that can melt PVC joints and create hazardous chemical burn risks for our arriving technicians.
  • Clear Service Pathways: Move vehicles from your front driveway and remove decorative pots or patio furniture around your exterior sewer cleanout to ensure our technician can maneuver camera diagnostic monitors and motorized cabling reels directly into position.
  • Contain Localized Spills: Place old towels around bathroom doors to prevent wastewater from reaching hardwood floors or bedroom carpets. Keep children and pets away from contaminated areas.

3. Long-Term Preventative Maintenance Guidance for Naples Homes

Southwest Florida's unique coastal geography requires proactive drainage maintenance:

  • Seasonal Snowbird Care: If departing Naples for the summer, pour non-toxic mineral oil into toilet bowls and floor drains to prevent P-trap water seals from evaporating during hot summer months.
  • Ficus & Palm Root Monitoring: Homes with extensive tropical landscaping should schedule preventative video camera scoping every 18 to 24 months before summer rains stimulate aggressive root growth.
  • Avoid "Flushable" Wipes: Even wipes advertised as flushable do not disintegrate in municipal sewer laterals. Dispose of wet wipes, paper towels, and hygiene products in trash receptacles.

5. Preventative Drainage Habits for Southwest Florida Residences

To keep your drains flowing smoothly and prevent future blockages:

  • Never Flush Wet Wipes: Even wipes marketed as "flushable" do not break down in municipal sewer laterals. They snag on pipe joints and root tendrils, creating dense clogs. Dispose of all wipes in waste bins.
  • Dispose of Cooking Fats Properly: Never pour bacon grease, cooking oil, or butter down kitchen garbage disposals. Pour warm grease into a metal can, let it solidify, and discard it in household trash.
  • Install Mesh Hair Catchers: Place stainless steel mesh strainers over shower and bathtub drains. Hair is the #1 cause of branch line bathroom stoppages in residential homes.
  • Inspect Exterior Cleanouts: Check your lawn cleanout cap every three months to verify that the threaded plug is tightly sealed, preventing lawn clippings, sand, and rainwater from entering your sewer line.
